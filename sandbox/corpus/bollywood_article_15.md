# Article 15 (2019)

## Overview
Article 15 is a 2019 Indian Hindi-language crime drama film directed by Anubhav Sinha. The film stars Ayushmann Khurrana as Ayan Ranjan, an IPS officer who investigates the rape and murder of two Dalit girls in rural Uttar Pradesh. The title refers to Article 15 of the Indian Constitution, which prohibits discrimination on the grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. The film is loosely inspired by the 2014 Badaun gang rape case.

## Plot
Ayan Ranjan, a young IPS officer educated abroad with a progressive outlook, is posted to a small town in Uttar Pradesh. He arrives to find two teenage Dalit girls murdered — hung from a tree — and a third girl missing. The investigation is stalled by caste interests: the local police, landowners, and politicians all have stakes in suppressing the case. Ayan's attempts to pursue justice are met with institutional resistance, social pressure, and personal threat.

The film unfolds as a police procedural that is simultaneously a reckoning with caste — its violence, its ordinariness, and its deep embeddedness in rural Indian life. Ayan, himself from a privileged caste, must confront his own class privilege and the limits of his understanding of a world he thought he could fix with good intentions alone.

## Themes
- **Caste discrimination and violence**: The film does not flinch from depicting the specific, systemic violence done to Dalit communities
- **Institutional corruption**: How police, politics, and caste elites collude to protect each other
- **Upper-caste awakening**: Ayan's education — not just intellectual but moral — as he confronts his own privilege
- **Article 15 as unfulfilled promise**: The constitutional guarantee of equality versus the lived reality of caste hierarchy
- **Class conflict**: The economic power differentials that underpin caste violence

## Critical Reception
Article 15 was widely praised as one of the most politically courageous mainstream Bollywood films in years. Critics celebrated its unflinching examination of caste violence, Ayushmann Khurrana's nuanced performance, and Anubhav Sinha's shift from commercial entertainment to social realism.
