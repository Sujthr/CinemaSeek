# Marriage Story (2019)

## Overview
Marriage Story is a 2019 American drama film written and directed by Noah Baumbach. The film stars Adam Driver as Charlie, a New York theatre director, and Scarlett Johansson as Nicole, an actress who was his muse. The film received six Academy Award nominations and won Best Original Score. It was released on Netflix and became one of the most discussed films of its year.

## Plot
Charlie and Nicole are separating. The film opens with each listing what they love about the other — voiceovers over images of intimacy and care. Then the marriage counsellor asks them to read the lists to each other, and Nicole cannot. The film follows their divorce proceedings across both coasts — Nicole returns to Los Angeles to shoot a pilot; Charlie remains in New York with their theatre company.

As the divorce proceeds and lawyers become involved, what was intended to be civil and humane becomes adversarial. The film's centrepiece is a single apartment scene — an argument that escalates from frustration to cruelty to screaming to physical aggression to tears — that is one of the most naked and devastating sequences of marital dissolution in cinema. Adam Driver sings a Sondheim song alone in an apartment. Scarlett Johansson sings "Being Alive." The film ends with Charlie moving to LA, tying Nicole's shoelace, the love between them transformed but not extinguished.

## Themes
- **The dissolution of love**: How a marriage can end not through cruelty but through drift and incompatibility
- **Institutional machinery vs human need**: The divorce legal process as a mechanism that converts private grief into adversarial strategy
- **Ensemble family dynamics**: The child Henry as the pivot around whom both parents orbit and for whom they must ultimately find coexistence
- **Performance and authenticity**: Both Charlie and Nicole are performers; the film interrogates how performance shapes even private relationships
- **Bittersweet endings**: The film ends on a note of earned, honest sadness rather than either closure or reconciliation
