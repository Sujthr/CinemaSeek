# Anurag Kashyap — Director Profile

## Overview
Anurag Kashyap (born 10 September 1972) is an Indian filmmaker, screenwriter, and actor regarded as the most important figure in contemporary independent Indian cinema. His films are characterised by their unflinching portrayal of violence, moral complexity, and the subterranean world of urban and semi-urban India. He is the director of Gangs of Wasseypur, Black Friday, Dev.D, Ugly, and co-founder of production house Phantom Films.

## Signature Style

### Gritty Realism and India's Underbelly
Kashyap's films are committed to portraying the India that Bollywood habitually ignores: the criminal ecosystems of UP and Bihar, the chawls of Dharavi, the underground rave culture of Mumbai, the feudal power structures of north Indian small towns. He shoots on location in actual spaces, casts non-professional or character actors alongside stars, and insists on dialectal authenticity.

Where mainstream Bollywood mythologises urban India as aspirational, Kashyap presents it as it is: contingent, violent, morally unresolved.

### Documentary Texture and Handheld Cameras
Kashyap's visual style is deliberately impure. He prefers handheld cameras that follow action rather than control it, creating a documentary-adjacent texture that distances his work from the formal elegance of art cinema and the glossy composure of mainstream Bollywood. This aesthetic choice is a moral one: Kashyap's gaze does not aestheticise poverty or violence; it witnesses them.

### Non-Linear Structure and Epic Scope
In Gangs of Wasseypur, Kashyap works across decades and generations, using non-linear flashbacks and multiple protagonist perspectives to construct a systemic portrait of power and violence. He is not interested in individual heroes but in ecosystems — how violence propagates through families, economies, and communities.

## Recurring Themes
- **Power, hierarchy, and feudal structures**: Kashyap's villains are never simply evil; they are products of specific economic and social arrangements
- **The costs of vengeance**: His films follow revenge cycles to their nihilistic conclusions — revenge does not liberate, it perpetuates
- **Moral ambiguity**: There are no clean heroes in Kashyap's films; every protagonist is implicated in the system they navigate
- **Youth and disillusionment**: Coming-of-age in Kashyap's films means encountering the gap between aspiration and reality
- **Class conflict and immobility**: The grinding impossibility of class ascent in stratified India

## Filmography Overview
Paanch (unreleased), Black Friday (2004), Dev.D (2009), Gulaal (2009), That Girl in Yellow Boots (2011), Gangs of Wasseypur Parts 1 & 2 (2012), Ugly (2013), Bombay Velvet (2015), Mukkabaaz (2017), Dobaaraa (2022)

## Critical Reception
Kashyap is the most internationally recognised Indian filmmaker of his generation. His films have screened at Cannes, Berlin, and Toronto. Gangs of Wasseypur is regularly cited on lists of the greatest films of the 21st century.
