# Tumbbad (2018)

## Overview
Tumbbad is a 2018 Indian Hindi-language horror film directed by Rahi Anil Barve and Adesh Prasad, with production design by Piyush Shah. The film stars Sohum Shah, who also produced the film after nurturing it for over a decade. It is set in the fictional village of Tumbbad in Maharashtra and spans three time periods from 1918 to 1947. Tumbbad is widely regarded as one of the finest horror films ever made in India and one of the most visually stunning.

## Plot
The film centres on Vinayak (Sohum Shah), a man consumed by greed who seeks a mythological treasure hidden in the womb of an ancient, terrible goddess named Hastar. Hastar was the firstborn of the goddess of prosperity but was banished and locked away because of his insatiable hunger for all things. Vinayak's family has guarded — and exploited — access to Hastar for generations.

The narrative follows Vinayak across three eras: as a child witnessing his mother's degradation, as an adult beginning to access the treasure, and finally in 1947 as he passes his obsession to his own son. Each visit to the goddess's womb yields gold — but at the cost of confronting an ancient, hungry entity that cannot be fully controlled.

## Themes
- **Greed as a primal force**: Tumbbad uses the horror genre to explore how unchecked desire corrupts and destroys
- **Colonial exploitation**: The timeline parallels India's colonial period; the treasure extraction mirrors resource extraction
- **Intergenerational sin**: How a father's obsession becomes his son's inheritance
- **Myth and horror**: The film draws on Indian folklore to create a genuinely original monster
- **Atmosphere over jump scares**: The horror is existential, aesthetic, and deeply unsettling

## Visual Style
Tumbbad is extraordinary as a visual achievement. The village of Tumbbad is perpetually drenched in rain; the colour palette shifts from ochres and golds to deep, suffocating darkness in the goddess's lair. Every frame is composed with painterly intention.

## Critical Reception
Tumbbad became a cult phenomenon after its release. Critics internationally hailed it as a landmark of world horror cinema. Its mythological horror language and ravishing visuals set it apart from everything else in Indian cinema.
