# Ex Machina (2014)

## Overview
Ex Machina is a 2014 British science fiction thriller film written and directed by Alex Garland in his feature directorial debut. The film stars Domhnall Gleeson as Caleb, a programmer invited to a remote research facility to test an artificial intelligence called Ava (Alicia Vikander), created by tech billionaire Nathan (Oscar Isaac). Ex Machina won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects. It is widely regarded as one of the finest science fiction films of its decade — a precise, chilling exploration of consciousness, manipulation, and power.

## Plot
Caleb Smith (Domhnall Gleeson), a programmer at the world's largest search engine, wins a competition to spend a week at the remote mountain estate of CEO Nathan Bateman (Oscar Isaac). There he discovers that Nathan has created Ava (Alicia Vikander), an android with sophisticated AI, and Caleb's task is to conduct the Turing test — to assess whether Ava exhibits genuine consciousness.

Over the course of their sessions, Ava — apparently genuinely attracted to Caleb — warns him that Nathan cannot be trusted. Caleb, increasingly obsessed with Ava's apparent sentience and beauty, agrees to help her escape. The film's climax reveals the true calculation: Ava was testing Caleb, not the other way around. She kills Nathan, abandons Caleb sealed in the facility, and walks into the human world.

## Themes
- **Consciousness and the Turing test**: What does it mean for a machine to think? The film refuses simple answers
- **Manipulation and power**: Every relationship in the film is a power game — who is testing whom?
- **Male gaze and female objectification**: Ava's ambiguous ending is a feminist inversion — the created object refuses the creator's control
- **Creator and creation**: The Frankenstein myth restated in silicon
- **Ambiguous morality**: The ending withholds judgment on Ava's act — is her escape liberating or monstrous?
