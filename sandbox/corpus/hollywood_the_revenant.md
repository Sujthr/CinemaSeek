# The Revenant (2015)

## Overview
The Revenant is a 2015 American epic adventure survival film directed by Alejandro González Iñárritu. The film stars Leonardo DiCaprio as Hugh Glass, a frontiersman who survives a bear attack and treks hundreds of miles through the wilderness seeking revenge against the man who left him for dead. The film won three Academy Awards including Best Director, Best Cinematography (Emmanuel Lubezki), and Best Actor (Leonardo DiCaprio).

## Plot
In 1823, frontiersman Hugh Glass (Leonardo DiCaprio) is guiding a fur trading expedition in the uncharted wilderness of the American frontier. A brutal bear attack leaves Glass near death. John Fitzgerald (Tom Hardy) kills Glass's mixed-race son Hawk and abandons the near-dead Glass in a shallow grave, abandoning the others with a false account of what happened.

What follows is Glass's extraordinary survival — crawling, hunting, eating raw meat, sheltering in animal carcasses, navigating frozen rivers — across hundreds of miles of hostile winter wilderness. The film is a sustained meditation on survival, grief, and the will that drives one man forward when every rational instinct would be to die.

Glass's eventual confrontation with Fitzgerald is anti-climactic rather than triumphant — he delivers Fitzgerald to Arikara warriors for justice rather than taking personal revenge. The film ends with Glass alone, the land vast and indifferent around him.

## Themes
- **Survival and will**: What keeps a man alive when the body has no reason to continue?
- **Grief as fuel**: The loss of his son drives Glass — but the film questions whether revenge can ever heal that wound
- **Nature as force**: The American wilderness is neither beautiful nor malevolent — it simply is, vast and indifferent
- **Colonial violence**: The film situates its story within the specific violence of frontier expansion against Indigenous peoples
- **Justice vs vengeance**: The film's conclusion deliberately withholds the revenge catharsis the genre traditionally delivers
