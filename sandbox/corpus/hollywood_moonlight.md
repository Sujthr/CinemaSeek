# Moonlight (2016)

## Overview
Moonlight is a 2016 American coming-of-age drama film written and directed by Barry Jenkins, based on Tarell Alvin McCraney's unpublished semi-autobiographical play "In Moonlight Black Boys Look Blue." The film stars Trevante Rhodes, André Holland, Janelle Monáe, and Mahershala Ali. It won the Academy Award for Best Picture, Best Supporting Actor (Mahershala Ali), and Best Adapted Screenplay, becoming the first film with a predominantly Black cast and the first LGBTQ film to win Best Picture.

## Plot
Moonlight is told in three chapters, each following a different era in the life of Chiron, a Black man growing up in the Liberty City neighborhood of Miami.

**Chapter 1 — "Little"**: As a young boy nicknamed "Little" (Alex R. Hibbert), Chiron is bullied for his sensitive nature. He is taken in by Juan (Mahershala Ali), a drug dealer, and his girlfriend Teresa (Janelle Monáe), who provide him with the warm, safe environment his crack-addicted mother cannot. Juan's tender mentorship — and his later death, felt rather than shown — haunts the rest of the film.

**Chapter 2 — "Chiron"**: As a teenager (Ashton Sanders), Chiron navigates school bullying, his mother's addiction, and his own emerging sexuality. A tentative romantic encounter with his friend Kevin leaves an indelible mark. A brutal assault, and its aftermath, force Chiron to reinvent himself.

**Chapter 3 — "Black"**: As an adult (Trevante Rhodes), Chiron has transformed himself into a hard, muscular drug dealer — performing the exact masculinity that once oppressed him. An unexpected phone call from Kevin initiates a reckoning.

## Themes
- **Identity and performance of masculinity**: How Chiron constructs and inhabits different identities to survive
- **Sexuality and silence**: The film portrays LGBTQ experience through what is not said as much as what is
- **Coming of age and trauma**: How childhood wounds shape adult selfhood
- **Mentorship and found family**: Juan and Teresa as substitute parents who give Chiron something his biological family cannot
- **Race and poverty**: The specific pressures of growing up Black and poor in America
- **Ambiguous resolution**: The film ends in a suspended moment of vulnerability and recognition that resists easy closure

## Directorial Style
Barry Jenkins' direction is impressionistic and intimate. He favours close-ups — eyes, skin, hands — and long moments of held silence. The cinematography (James Laxton) uses blues, pinks, and purples that echo the film's title. The score by Nicholas Britell blends hip-hop and classical.
