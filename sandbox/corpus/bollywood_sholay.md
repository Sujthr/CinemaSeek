# Sholay (1975)

## Overview
Sholay is a 1975 Indian Hindi-language action adventure film directed by Ramesh Sippy and produced by G.P. Sippy. The film stars Dharmendra, Amitabh Bachchan, Sanjeev Kumar, Hema Malini, Jaya Bhaduri, and Amjad Khan as the iconic villain Gabbar Singh. Often called the "greatest Indian film ever made," Sholay ran continuously in a Mumbai cinema for over five years and remains a defining cultural text of Indian popular culture.

## Plot
Thakur Baldev Singh (Sanjeev Kumar), a retired police officer, hires two petty criminals — Jai (Amitabh Bachchan) and Veeru (Dharmendra) — to capture the feared dacoit Gabbar Singh (Amjad Khan), who had attacked his family and severed his arms. The two friends arrive in the village of Ramgarh, where they integrate into the community, fall in love, and eventually face Gabbar in a final reckoning.

Gabbar Singh is the film's dominating presence — a roaring, merciless bandit whose lines ("Kitne aadmi the?") became part of everyday Indian vocabulary. The film builds to a climax in which justice is rendered not through the legal system but through personal vengeance — a morally complex resolution that the censor board controversially altered.

## Themes
- **Male friendship and camaraderie**: Jai and Veeru's friendship is the emotional heart of the film
- **Revenge and justice**: The limits of institutional law in a lawless landscape
- **Good vs evil**: The film's moral universe is starkly drawn, with Gabbar as a figure of pure menace
- **Community and protection**: The responsibility of the strong to protect the vulnerable
- **Sacrifice**: Jai's death is one of the most emotionally resonant moments in Indian cinema

## Legacy
Sholay is the prototype of the "masala" film — blending action, comedy, romance, drama, and tragedy into a seamless entertainment. Its influence on subsequent Indian cinema is immeasurable.
