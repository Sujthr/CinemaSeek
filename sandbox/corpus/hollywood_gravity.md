# Gravity (2013)

## Overview
Gravity is a 2013 science fiction thriller film directed by Alfonso Cuarón and co-written with his son Jonás Cuarón. The film stars Sandra Bullock as Dr. Ryan Stone, a medical engineer on her first space shuttle mission, and George Clooney as veteran astronaut Matt Kowalski. Gravity won seven Academy Awards including Best Director, Best Cinematography, Best Film Editing, and Best Visual Effects. It is one of the most technically accomplished films in cinema history.

## Plot
During a spacewalk to service the Hubble Space Telescope, debris from a Russian satellite strike destroys the shuttle Explorer, killing most of the crew. Stone (Sandra Bullock) and Kowalski (George Clooney) are stranded in orbit with depleting oxygen, no communication with Earth, and no immediate rescue. Kowalski must reach the International Space Station before they run out of fuel.

After Kowalski sacrifices himself to give Stone enough momentum to reach the ISS, Stone faces a series of increasingly desperate situations alone — fire, equipment failure, loss of propulsion — as she attempts to reach the Chinese space station Tiangong and use its emergency return capsule.

The film is fundamentally a survival story but also a rebirth narrative: Stone, we learn, has been emotionally dead since the accidental death of her daughter. Space becomes the crucible in which she chooses — consciously, against every obstacle — to live.

## Themes
- **Survival and rebirth**: The film is explicitly a story of a woman choosing to want to live
- **Isolation**: The absolute silence and emptiness of space as a mirror for emotional withdrawal
- **Technology and human fragility**: The elaborate systems that sustain life in space — and their vulnerability
- **The long take as tension**: Cuarón's extended takes (some over 13 minutes) immerse the viewer in the continuous experience of danger

## Critical Reception
Gravity was a technical landmark and a massive commercial success. Its long-take opening sequence and continuous tension were universally praised.
