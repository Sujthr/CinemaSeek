# Mughal-E-Azam (1960)

## Overview
Mughal-E-Azam is a 1960 Indian epic historical drama film written and directed by K. Asif. The film stars Prithviraj Kapoor as the Mughal Emperor Akbar, Dilip Kumar as Prince Salim, and Madhubala as the court dancer Anarkali. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest Indian films ever made and holds the distinction of being the first Indian film to be released in colour after a black-and-white theatrical run (it was digitally colourised and re-released in 2004).

## Plot
Set in the court of Emperor Akbar (Prithviraj Kapoor), the film tells the legendary story of the doomed love between Prince Salim (Dilip Kumar) and Anarkali (Madhubala), a slave girl and court dancer. Their love defies royal protocol and Akbar's absolute authority. When the Emperor learns of the relationship, he orders Anarkali imprisoned. Salim rebels against his father, leading to a confrontation between the Mughal army and Salim's forces.

The climax pits royal authority against the power of love. Akbar gives Anarkali a choice — renounce Salim or watch him executed. She chooses to sacrifice herself to save him, pretending to poison Salim's drink, and is sentenced to be buried alive within a wall. The film ends on a note of sacrifice, tragedy, and parental love — Akbar, moved by Anarkali's devotion, secretly arranges her escape while publicly maintaining the execution.

## Themes
- **Love vs duty**: The conflict between romantic love and filial/civic obligation
- **Power and absolute authority**: Akbar as a figure who loves his son but cannot bend the laws he has made
- **Sacrifice and tragedy**: Anarkali's selfless love expressed through self-abnegation
- **Court life and hierarchy**: The rigid social order of the Mughal court
- **Beauty as tragedy**: Anarkali's extraordinary beauty and artistry as the cause of her destruction

## Cultural Legacy
Mughal-E-Azam is the definitive statement of Indian period cinema. Its scale was unprecedented — the production took nine years to complete. Madhubala's performance as Anarkali remains one of the most iconic in Indian cinema history.
