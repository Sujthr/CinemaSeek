# Parasite (2019)

## Overview
Parasite (Korean: Gisaengchung) is a 2019 South Korean black comedy thriller film written and directed by Bong Joon-ho. The film stars Song Kang-ho, Lee Sun-kyun, Cho Yeo-jeong, Choi Woo-shik, and Park So-dam. It made history by becoming the first non-English-language film to win the Academy Award for Best Picture, also winning Best Director, Best Original Screenplay, and Best International Feature Film.

## Plot
The Kim family — father Ki-taek (Song Kang-ho), mother Chung-sook (Jang Hye-jin), son Ki-woo (Choi Woo-shik), and daughter Ki-jung (Park So-dam) — are a poor, semi-employed family living in a cramped semi-basement apartment in Seoul. Through a series of elaborate ruses, each family member infiltrates the wealthy Park household, posing as unrelated professionals: Ki-woo as an English tutor, Ki-jung as an art therapist, Ki-taek as a driver, Chung-sook as a housekeeper.

The Kims enjoy their new positions, living vicariously through the Parks' luxury. Then, during a rainy night when the Parks are away, a secret is discovered in the Park house — a previous housekeeper's husband has been living in a hidden basement bunker for years. This discovery upends everything. The film escalates from dark comedy to horror to tragedy in its third act, culminating in a daylight party massacre and an act of impulsive, class-fuelled violence.

The film ends with an ambiguous, melancholy epilogue — Ki-woo imagines earning enough money to buy the Park house and free his father from the bunker where he is now hiding. The audience understands this is fantasy, not plan.

## Themes
- **Class conflict and wealth inequality**: The film is a meticulously designed study of how economic inequality shapes every interaction between the Kims and the Parks
- **The geography of class**: The spatial metaphor is central — the Parks live on a hill, the Kims in a semi-basement, the hidden man below ground entirely
- **Resentment and aspiration**: The seductiveness of the Parks' world versus the violence it eventually generates
- **Dark comedy and tragedy**: Bong Joon-ho uses class-driven absurdity to generate both laughter and dread
- **The parasite metaphor**: Both families are parasites in different ways — one feeding on the other's labour, one inhabiting their host's world

## Critical Reception
Parasite was one of the most acclaimed films of its decade. Critics praised its structural perfection, its tonal audacity, and Bong Joon-ho's control. Its Oscar sweep was celebrated as a historic moment for non-English-language cinema globally.
