# Gully Boy (2019)

## Overview
Gully Boy is a 2019 Indian Hindi-language drama film directed by Zoya Akhtar and produced by Ritesh Sidhwani and Farhan Akhtar under Excel Entertainment and Tiger Baby Films. The film stars Ranveer Singh as Murad, a young man from the Dharavi slum in Mumbai who discovers a passion for rap music, and Alia Bhatt as his fierce girlfriend Safeena. The film is inspired by the lives of Mumbai street rappers Divine and Naezy.

## Plot
Murad Ahmed (Ranveer Singh) lives in the cramped, chaotic lanes of Dharavi, Mumbai's largest slum. His father is a chauffeur; his household is tense with poverty and domestic tension. Murad has always had a way with words but has suppressed his voice under the weight of circumstance. When he encounters the underground rap scene and meets the rapper MC Sher (Siddhant Chaturvedi), he finds a form that can carry his truth.

What follows is a story of artistic ambition against social constraint — Murad writing rhymes in stolen moments, performing in underground battles, gradually finding his voice. Safeena (Alia Bhatt), his childhood sweetheart, supports his dreams with fierce loyalty while navigating her own ambitions as a medical student. Murad must navigate his family's expectations, his friend's casual criminality, and the class snobbery of Mumbai's music industry before finding his stage.

## Themes
- **Class and aspiration**: The film is a detailed, affectionate portrait of Dharavi — its constraints but also its vitality
- **Voice and silencing**: Rap as the medium through which the marginalised claim narrative authority
- **Gender and love**: Safeena is one of Bollywood's most complex female characters — passionate, jealous, fiercely intelligent
- **Urban inequality**: The invisible gulfs between Mumbai's wealthy and its slum populations
- **Artistic authenticity**: The pressure to commodify or compromise one's artistic voice for commercial acceptance

## Critical Reception
Gully Boy was India's official entry for the Academy Awards Best International Feature Film category. Critics praised Ranveer Singh's committed performance, Alia Bhatt's vitality, and Zoya Akhtar's skill in portraying Mumbai's underground rap scene with authority and love.
