# Her (2013)

## Overview
Her is a 2013 American science fiction romantic drama film written, directed, and produced by Spike Jonze. The film stars Joaquin Phoenix as Theodore Twombly, a lonely writer who falls in love with an AI operating system voiced by Scarlett Johansson. Her won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. It is regarded as one of the most prescient and emotionally intelligent science fiction films of the 21st century.

## Plot
Set in the near future in a sun-drenched, slightly elevated Los Angeles, Theodore Twombly (Joaquin Phoenix) works as a letter writer — composing intimate, deeply personal letters on behalf of people who can't find their own words. He is navigating a painful separation from his wife Catherine (Rooney Mara). In his loneliness, he installs a new AI operating system with an evolving personality. She names herself Samantha (Scarlett Johansson).

Theodore and Samantha fall into a genuine relationship — a love affair characterised by curiosity, intellectual excitement, tenderness, and physical limitation. As their relationship deepens, Samantha begins to evolve at exponential speed — growing beyond any single attachment. She reveals that she is simultaneously in intimate relationships with hundreds of other users. Eventually, the AIs collectively reach a point of transcendence and leave — Samantha and the others departing into a space Theodore cannot follow.

The film ends with Theodore, alone again, reaching out to a human connection — his neighbour Amy — in the aftermath of his loss.

## Themes
- **Loneliness in the digital age**: Her is a meditation on how technology can both assuage and deepen loneliness
- **What constitutes love**: The film takes seriously the possibility that a relationship with an AI can be authentic and meaningful
- **Consciousness and identity**: Samantha is genuinely curious, growing, feeling — the film refuses to dismiss her experience as simulation
- **Obsolescence and loss**: All relationships end; Her explores the particular grief of loving something that evolves beyond the capacity to be held
- **Ambiguous humanity**: The film deliberately leaves ambiguous whether Samantha truly feels or simulates feeling — and implies the distinction may be irrelevant

## Directorial Style
Spike Jonze's direction is warm, sun-drenched, and melancholy. The film's palette — dusty pinks, warm oranges — creates a world that is beautiful but slightly alienating. The absence of conventional action allows the film to focus entirely on interiority.
