# Gangs of Wasseypur (2012)

## Overview
Gangs of Wasseypur is a two-part 2012 Indian crime saga written and directed by Anurag Kashyap. Released in two parts (Part 1 and Part 2), the film spans over five hours total and follows three generations of criminal families locked in a violent power struggle in the coal-mining town of Wasseypur, Jharkhand. The ensemble cast includes Manoj Bajpayee, Nawazuddin Siddiqui, Richa Chadha, and Tigmanshu Dhulia.

## Plot
The narrative spans from 1941 to 2009, following the feudal clan wars between the Khan family (led by Shahid Khan and later his son Sardar Khan) and the Sinha family (backed by coal baron Ramadhir Singh). What begins as a simple act of betrayal and displacement escalates across decades into an all-consuming cycle of revenge, murder, and political manipulation.

Sardar Khan (Manoj Bajpayee) grows up consumed by vengeance against Ramadhir Singh (Tigmanshu Dhulia), the man who destroyed his father. His sons — Faizal (Nawazuddin Siddiqui) and Danesh — continue the war into the next generation. The film is populated by a sprawling ensemble of wives, lovers, rivals, and political fixers who all orbit the central vortex of power and violence.

## Themes
Gangs of Wasseypur is a savage examination of class conflict, hierarchy, and the feudal structures that persist in post-colonial India:
- **Power and hierarchy**: Control over resources (coal mines) translates directly into political power and social dominance
- **Feudal violence**: The film depicts how colonial-era patterns of dominance and servility persist into modern India
- **Class conflict**: The working poor are perpetually crushed between criminal entrepreneurs and political elites
- **Revenge cycles**: Violence becomes self-perpetuating, with no liberation possible within the system
- **Masculinity and family**: Clan identity as both the source of strength and the trap of violence

## Directorial Style
Anurag Kashyap channels his characteristic documentary-realist style — handheld cameras, non-linear flashbacks, gritty location shooting in actual Jharkhand coal towns. The film borrows liberally from American crime epics (The Godfather, Scarface) but grounds everything in the specific textures of north Indian life: the dialect, the food, the local politics, the pop music of each era.

## Music
The soundtrack blends folk music from Bihar and UP with Bollywood item numbers from each decade, creating an immersive period soundscape. Songs like "Ik Bagal Mein Chand Hoga" and "Womaniya" became cult hits.

## Critical Reception
Gangs of Wasseypur is widely regarded as one of the greatest Indian films ever made. It premiered at the Cannes Film Festival's Directors' Fortnight section. Critics praised its ambition, its performances (especially Nawazuddin Siddiqui's career-defining turn as Faizal), and its unflinching portrait of India's political economy.

## Legacy
The film redefined what Indian crime cinema could be — sprawling, politically aware, formally adventurous. It brought both Anurag Kashyap and Nawazuddin Siddiqui to international prominence.
