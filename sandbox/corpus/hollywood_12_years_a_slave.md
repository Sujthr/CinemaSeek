# 12 Years a Slave (2013)

## Overview
12 Years a Slave is a 2013 British-American historical drama film directed by Steve McQueen and adapted by John Ridley from Solomon Northup's 1853 memoir. The film stars Chiwetel Ejiofor as Solomon Northup, a free Black man kidnapped and sold into slavery. Supporting roles are played by Michael Fassbender, Benedict Cumberbatch, Paul Dano, Lupita Nyong'o, and Brad Pitt. It won three Academy Awards including Best Picture, Best Supporting Actress (Lupita Nyong'o), and Best Adapted Screenplay.

## Plot
Solomon Northup (Chiwetel Ejiofor) is a free Black man and talented violinist living in Saratoga, New York in 1841. Tricked by two men who promise him work as a musician in Washington D.C., he is drugged, chained, and sold into slavery in the Deep South under the name "Platt." He spends twelve years enslaved under two very different masters: the relatively moderate Ford (Benedict Cumberbatch) and the sadistic, sexually predatory Edwin Epps (Michael Fassbender).

The film does not flinch from the reality of slavery's brutality — floggings, sexual violence, starvation, and the grinding psychological violence of dehumanisation. Solomon's survival requires suppressing his true identity, waiting for an opportunity. That opportunity arrives through a chance encounter with a Canadian carpenter (Brad Pitt) who agrees to contact Solomon's family in the North.

## Themes
- **Class and racial hierarchy**: The film depicts slavery as an economic and social system — its machinery, its legal protections, its complicity
- **Dehumanisation and resilience**: How Solomon maintains his internal selfhood under conditions designed to erase it
- **The spectacle of suffering**: Steve McQueen refuses to look away — long, unedited takes force audiences to witness rather than observe
- **White complicity**: The film implicates the spectrum of white response, from active cruelty to passive collaboration
- **Identity under erasure**: Solomon's forbidden true name as the central site of resistance
