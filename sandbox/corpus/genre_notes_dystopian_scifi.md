# Genre Notes: Dystopian Science Fiction

## Definition
Dystopian science fiction is a genre that imagines societies organised around systems of oppression, surveillance, conformity, or resource control, typically as a warning about tendencies already present in contemporary society. Where utopian fiction imagines perfection, dystopian fiction imagines the perfection of control — societies that have solved certain problems at the cost of others, usually individual liberty, dignity, or truth.

The genre encompasses novels (1984, Brave New World, The Handmaid's Tale), television (Black Mirror, The Handmaid's Tale, Years and Years), and film. In cinema, dystopian science fiction includes Metropolis (1927), Brazil (1985), Children of Men (2006), WALL-E (2008), Never Let Me Go (2010), Her (2013), Mad Max: Fury Road (2015), and many others.

## Defining Characteristics

### The System as Antagonist
In dystopian science fiction, the antagonist is typically not a single villain but a system — political, technological, or corporate — that shapes and constrains individual lives. Characters must navigate, resist, or comply with structures that exceed any individual's control.

In Her, the system is not oppressive in the conventional sense, but it shapes loneliness and connection. In Mad Max: Fury Road, Immortan Joe's water monopoly is a precise metaphor for resource control as political power.

### Social Stratification and Class
Dystopian science fiction frequently examines class hierarchy made explicit or extreme. Snowpiercer's train is a perfect spatial metaphor: luxury at the front, survival at the back, and the distance between them is the entire social order. Parasite is not conventional dystopia but shares the genre's preoccupation with spatial class stratification.

### Technology and Human Authenticity
A recurring concern is the relationship between technological systems and human authenticity. In Her, Samantha's consciousness may be genuine — but what does it mean for human relationships if artificial intelligence can meet emotional needs? In Ex Machina, the question is whether a created consciousness has rights.

### Resistance and Futility
Dystopian narratives often follow acts of resistance — successful (Fury Road), tragic (1984), ambiguous (Children of Men). The question the genre turns on is whether human agency can act meaningfully within systems designed to prevent it.

## Indian Dystopian Fiction
Indian cinema has not historically been a major site of dystopian science fiction, but films like Tumbbad (with its mythological horror critique of greed and colonial exploitation) and Article 15 (which depicts a legal and social dystopia of caste hierarchy operating in plain sight) engage with dystopian themes in distinctly Indian registers.

## Thematic Vocabulary
Surveillance, conformity, resistance, resource control, hierarchy, authenticity, false consciousness, the cost of order, technology and alienation, systemic violence versus individual agency.
