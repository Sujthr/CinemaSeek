# Whiplash (2014)

## Overview
Whiplash is a 2014 American drama film written and directed by Damien Chazelle in his feature directorial debut. The film stars Miles Teller as Andrew Neiman, a young jazz drummer at the Shaffer Conservatory in New York, and J.K. Simmons as Terence Fletcher, his terrifying instructor. Whiplash won three Academy Awards including Best Supporting Actor for J.K. Simmons, Best Film Editing, and Best Sound Mixing.

## Plot
Andrew Neiman (Miles Teller) is a dedicated first-year drumming student at the Shaffer Conservatory who dreams of being one of the jazz greats. He is recruited into the studio band of Terence Fletcher (J.K. Simmons), the conservatory's most feared and respected instructor. Fletcher's teaching method is one of psychological brutality — screaming, humiliating, physically threatening students in pursuit of musical perfection. His theory: the greatest musicians are those who refuse to be stopped even by the most ferocious discouragement.

Andrew, desperate to prove himself worthy of Fletcher's attention and approval, sacrifices his relationship, his health, and his sanity in pursuit of perfection. A car accident, a public humiliation, and a vicious betrayal lead to Andrew's expulsion and Fletcher's firing. The film's extraordinary climax — a concert performance that begins as a final humiliation by Fletcher and transforms into a virtuosic act of defiance — is one of the most electrifying sequences in recent American film.

## Themes
- **Ambition and obsession**: The film offers no easy answer about whether the cost of greatness is worth paying
- **Abuse of power**: Fletcher's methods are abusive and his reasoning — that pain produces greatness — is presented as both credible and monstrous
- **The ambiguous mentor**: Is Fletcher's cruelty destructive or transformative? The film refuses to resolve this
- **Perfectionism and self-destruction**: Andrew's injury, his drumming until he bleeds, his total social withdrawal
- **The price of excellence**: Coming-of-age as a story of self-annihilation in service of art

## Critical Reception
Whiplash was praised for its kinetic direction, its extraordinary performances, and its refusal of comfortable moral resolutions. The final drumming sequence is considered one of the most thrilling scene in contemporary American cinema.
