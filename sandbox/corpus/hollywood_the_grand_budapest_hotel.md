# The Grand Budapest Hotel (2014)

## Overview
The Grand Budapest Hotel is a 2014 adventure comedy film written and directed by Wes Anderson. The film features an ensemble cast including Ralph Fiennes, Tony Revolori, Saoirse Ronan, F. Murray Abraham, Mathieu Amalric, Adrien Brody, Willem Dafoe, Jeff Goldblum, Harvey Keitel, Jude Law, and Bill Murray. It won four Academy Awards including Best Production Design, Best Costume Design, Best Original Score, and Best Makeup. It is Anderson's most commercially successful film.

## Plot
Set primarily in the fictional European nation of Zubrowka during the 1930s, the film follows Gustave H. (Ralph Fiennes), the legendary concierge of the Grand Budapest Hotel, and his new lobby boy Zero Moustafa (Tony Revolori). When one of Gustave's elderly aristocratic female companions dies and leaves him a priceless painting, her scheming family (led by Adrien Brody) pursues him for it. Gustave is framed for murder, imprisoned, escapes, and eventually solves the mystery of the painting with Zero's help.

The frame narrative — the story of how Zero, now an old man (F. Murray Abraham), came to own the hotel — adds a layer of nostalgic elegy. The hotel's grandeur is already in decline; by the frame narrative's present day, it is a relic.

## Themes
- **Ensemble and character comedy**: Anderson orchestrates a vast cast of characters, each distinct, comic, and briefly vivid
- **Nostalgia and the death of elegance**: The film mourns a European world of manners and grandeur being obliterated by fascism and history
- **Loyalty and friendship**: Gustave and Zero's relationship — formally absurd, genuinely tender
- **Class and performance**: Gustave's extraordinary civility as both natural aristocracy and social performance
- **Historical violence under comic surface**: The fascist military encroaching on the story's margins is played for dark comedy but carries genuine menace

## Directorial Style
Wes Anderson's signature aesthetic — perfectly centred compositions, pastel palettes, intricate production design, storybook flatness — is at its most elaborate here. The film cycles through different aspect ratios for different time periods.
