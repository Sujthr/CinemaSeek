# Inception (2010)

## Overview
Inception is a 2010 science fiction action film written, produced, and directed by Christopher Nolan. The film stars Leonardo DiCaprio as Dom Cobb, a professional thief who steals information by entering people's dreams, and a supporting ensemble including Joseph Gordon-Levitt, Elliot Page, Tom Hardy, Ken Watanabe, and Cillian Murphy. Inception won four Academy Awards and became one of the highest-grossing films of 2010. It is among the defining films of Nolan's career and one of the most discussed films for its structural and thematic complexity.

## Plot
Dom Cobb (Leonardo DiCaprio) is an expert in "extraction" — stealing secrets from the subconscious while a subject dreams. He is a fugitive, unable to return to his children in America after being accused of his wife Mal's death. Saito (Ken Watanabe) offers him a chance at exoneration if he can perform "inception" — planting an idea in someone's mind rather than extracting one.

The target is Robert Fischer (Cillian Murphy), heir to a corporate empire his dying father founded. The idea to be planted: break up the company. Cobb assembles a team and they enter a dream within a dream within a dream — three nested levels of reality, each operating at different time scales. The mission is complicated by Cobb's own subconscious intrusions — projections of Mal (Marion Cotillard), his dead wife, who keep sabotaging the operation.

The film ends with Cobb reunited with his children. He spins his totem — a top that keeps spinning in dreams but falls in reality. The top spins — and the film cuts to black before it falls or doesn't. The ending is deliberately, definitively ambiguous.

## Themes
- **Non-linear time and temporal fragmentation**: Dreams operate at different time scales simultaneously; the narrative enacts this through multiple nested timelines
- **Memory and identity**: Cobb's subconscious is saturated with Mal — his guilt and grief shape his perception of reality itself
- **Subjective memory vs objective reality**: The film asks whether Cobb can trust his own perception, and by extension whether the audience can trust the film's
- **The ambiguous ending**: Inception's final shot is one of cinema's most discussed — does the top fall? Is Cobb dreaming? The film refuses to answer
- **Grief and letting go**: The emotional core is Cobb's inability to release his guilt and idealized memory of Mal

## Directorial Style
Nolan's direction is architectural — the nested dream structure requires extraordinary clarity of exposition. The film's action sequences (a zero-gravity hotel corridor fight, a snowy mountain fortress assault) are inventive and spatially thrilling.
