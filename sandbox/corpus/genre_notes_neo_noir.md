# Genre Notes: Neo-Noir

## Definition
Neo-noir is a revival and reinvention of the classical film noir tradition that dominated Hollywood in the 1940s and 1950s. While classical noir was defined by its visual style (high-contrast black-and-white cinematography, shadows, venetian blind light patterns), its narrative conventions (the femme fatale, the hardboiled detective, the doomed protagonist), and its moral atmosphere (moral ambiguity, fatalism, the corruption of the American dream), neo-noir extends, subverts, and updates these elements into new contexts.

Neo-noir emerged as a distinct form in the late 1960s and early 1970s with films like Chinatown (1974) and Taxi Driver (1976), and has continued to evolve through films like Blood Simple (1984), L.A. Confidential (1997), Mulholland Drive (2001), Zodiac (2007), Drive (2011), and Andhadhun (2018).

## Core Conventions

### Moral Ambiguity
Neo-noir is constitutively morally ambiguous. The protagonist is typically compromised — a detective who breaks the rules, a criminal who has a code, an ordinary person drawn into darkness. Good and evil are not cleanly distributed. In Andhadhun, the protagonist's fake blindness implicates him in what follows; his "innocence" is itself a kind of deception.

### The Femme Fatale Reinvented
Classical noir's femme fatale — a beautiful, dangerous woman who manipulates men to her ends — is reinvented in neo-noir with greater complexity. In Andhadhun, Simi (Tabu) is a brilliant, resourceful antagonist rather than a simply destructive force. The neo-noir femme fatale often has genuine motivations and genuine intelligence; she may even be the most capable person in the film.

### Urban Darkness and Setting
Neo-noir is typically urban — cities as spaces of danger, hidden knowledge, and moral corruption. The city contains worlds invisible to its ordinary inhabitants; the protagonist's journey is into those hidden spaces. In Indian neo-noir, this translates to the specific geography of Indian cities: the chawls of Mumbai, the corrupt provincial towns, the underground spaces beneath visible society.

### Non-Linear Structure and Revelation
Many neo-noir films use structural revelation — information is withheld from the audience and released strategically, forcing retrospective reinterpretation of what came before. In Andhadhun, the final revelation about what Akash actually saw (or chose to pretend not to see) reframes the entire film.

## Indian Neo-Noir
Bollywood has developed its own neo-noir tradition, particularly in the thriller films of Sriram Raghavan and in Anurag Kashyap's early work. Andhadhun is the defining example: a film that uses classical noir conventions (blind pianist, femme fatale, MacGuffin) in a thoroughly contemporary Indian context, with a tonal instability (dark comedy shading into thriller shading into horror) that is distinctly its own.

## Thematic Vocabulary
Fate, complicity, the corrupting power of desire, hidden knowledge, the city as moral labyrinth, ambiguity of guilt, the futility of virtue in a corrupt world.
