# Masaan (2015)

## Overview
Masaan is a 2015 Indian Hindi-language drama film written by Varun Grover and directed by Neeraj Ghaywan in his feature directorial debut. Set in Varanasi, the film follows two parallel stories of grief, shame, and tentative hope. It stars Richa Chadha, Vicky Kaushal, Sanjay Mishra, and Shweta Tripathi. Masaan won two awards at the Cannes Film Festival (Un Certain Regard section) — the FIPRESCI Prize and the Promising Future Award.

## Plot
The film weaves two narrative threads set against the sacred ghats of Varanasi, where the Ganges flows between life and death.

**First story**: Devi (Richa Chadha), a college girl, is caught in a hotel room with her boyfriend by the police. Her boyfriend commits suicide; Devi and her father are blackmailed. The film traces Devi's journey through shame, paralysis, and eventual, quiet defiance.

**Second story**: Deepak (Vicky Kaushal) is a young lower-caste man who falls in love with Shaalu (Shweta Tripathi), a girl from a higher caste. Their love is uncomplicated and genuine. When tragedy strikes, Deepak must find the will to continue. He works as a Dom — a member of the untouchable caste who manages cremations at the ghats — and his profession puts him in direct contact with death daily, even as he seeks to live.

Both stories are suffused with the specific atmosphere of Varanasi: the smoke of the funeral pyres, the chants on the river, the weight of tradition pressing down on those who want simply to be human.

## Themes
- **Caste and forbidden love**: The brutal social architecture that separates Deepak and Shaalu
- **Shame and female sexuality**: How women are punished for their bodies while men escape judgment
- **Grief and recovery**: The slow, non-linear process of surviving devastating loss
- **The sacred and the profane**: Varanasi as a space where life, death, desire, and duty collide
- **Class**: Both protagonists are trapped by social position — one by gender, one by caste

## Critical Reception
Masaan was rapturously received at Cannes and celebrated in India as a major work of independent cinema. Critics praised Ghaywan's assured debut direction, Richa Chadha's restrained intensity, and Vicky Kaushal's breakthrough performance.

**Year**: 2015  
**Cast**: Richa Chadha, Vicky Kaushal, Sanjay Mishra, Shweta Tripathi
